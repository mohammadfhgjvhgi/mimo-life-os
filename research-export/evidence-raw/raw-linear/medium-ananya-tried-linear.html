I Finally Tried Linear and Now I Get the Hype

Look, I’m not usually the person who jumps on every new productivity tool. I’ve been riding the Jira train for years (with varying levels of enthusiasm, if I’m being honest). But after seeing Linear mentioned in basically every engineering Discord and Twitter thread, I decided to actually give it a shot.

And yeah… I get it now.

What Even Is Linear?

Linear is an issue tracking tool built specifically for software teams. Think Jira, but if Jira was designed by people who actually enjoy using their tools. It’s focused on speed, simplicity, and just letting you get work done without clicking through seventeen menus.

The Things That Actually Matter

It’s ridiculously fast. Like, noticeably fast. You hit a keyboard shortcut, type what you need, and you’re done. No loading spinners, no waiting for pages to render. It sounds like a small thing until you’re creating your 50th ticket of the week.

Keyboard shortcuts everywhere. Press C to create an issue. Cmd+K for the command palette. You can literally navigate the entire app without touching your mouse. As someone who lives in VS Code, this feels natural.

The UI doesn’t make me want to cry. It’s clean, minimal, and everything is where you’d expect it to be. The design actually helps you focus instead of overwhelming you with options.

The Workflow Just… Works

Setting up cycles (their version of sprints) is straightforward. Moving issues between states is drag-and-drop simple. The roadmap view actually makes sense. It’s all the stuff you need without the enterprise bloat.

They also have this neat thing where issues automatically get IDs like ENG-123, which makes referencing them in PRs super clean. Small detail, but it matters.

Is It Perfect?

Nothing is. If you need complex project management with dependencies, Gantt charts, and resource allocation, you might still need something beefier. Linear is intentionally simple, which is its strength but also means it won’t solve every problem.

The pricing is also more on the premium side compared to some alternatives, though there’s a free tier for small teams.

Should You Try It?

If you’re on a team that values moving fast and you’re tired of wrestling with clunky tools, absolutely give it a shot. They have a generous free tier, so there’s no risk.

For me? I’m a convert. Our team switched over two months ago and I can’t imagine going back. The time we save on just basic issue management adds up fast.

Sometimes the hype is real. This is one of those times.
