# Pattern: Artifacts UX

> PAT-3. Evidence synthesis across 13 products. NO MiMo design. Pattern extraction only.

## 1. Pattern Definition

**Artifacts UX** refers to the design of dedicated, hoistable, persistent surfaces for AI- or user-generated content that is significant, self-contained, and likely to be edited, iterated, or reused outside the immediate conversational flow. The pattern's signature properties, observed across the evidence set, are:

1. **Hoisting** — content exceeding a threshold (ChatGPT: >10 lines; Claude: >15 lines) is lifted out of the chat thread into a dedicated side panel or workspace [Source: evidence/chatgpt.md §13, citing https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9930697-what-is-the-canvas-feature-in-chatgpt-and-how-do-i-use-it; Source: evidence/claude.md §13, citing https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9796387-what-are-artifacts].
2. **Type-aware rendering** — different artifact classes (Markdown doc, React/HTML component, SVG, Mermaid diagram, code, scheduled task, calendar event, daily note, whiteboard) receive purpose-specific editors and viewers rather than a uniform surface [Source: evidence/claude.md §13; evidence/obsidian.md §13; evidence/manus.md §13].
3. **Versioning + fork** — every artifact carries a version history and a fork/duplicate affordance that creates an independent copy (Lovable Remix, Bolt Duplicate/Transfer, v0 Fork, Heptabase sub-whiteboard) [Source: evidence/lovable.md §13; evidence/bolt.md §13; evidence/v0.md §13; evidence/heptabase.md §13].
4. **Share/Publish** — a first-class externalization surface: a public URL, embeddable iframe, or published webpage (Claude `claude.site/public/artifacts/<UUID>/embed`; Manus auto-publish; Craft published documents with custom domains; Things3 has no share surface — confirming the property is selective, not universal) [Source: evidence/claude.md §13; evidence/manus.md §13; evidence/craft.md §13].
5. **Direct-edit + AI-suggest duality** — the user can directly click into the artifact and type, AND the AI can propose structured edits (ChatGPT Suggest edits; v0 Design Mode apply commits; Lovable Visual Edits; Craft Assistant Explore mode) [Source: evidence/chatgpt.md §13; evidence/v0.md §13; evidence/lovable.md §13; evidence/craft.md §13].

## 2. Why It Matters (with academic evidence)

Artifacts matter because they directly attack two forms of **extraneous cognitive load** (CLT, Sweller 1988) that pure-chat interfaces impose: (a) split-attention between scrolling chat history and the content being edited, and (b) transient working-memory loss when content of significant length scrolls out of the visible viewport. By hoisting content >10–15 lines into a stable side panel, artifacts convert an in-thread transient into a persistent workspace, freeing working memory (capacity ~4±1 chunks per Cowan 2001) for the editing task itself [Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §1, citing https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_load; Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §30, citing Chandler & Sweller 1991 on the split-attention effect].

The pattern also aligns with **Norman's conceptual-model principle**: a separate artifact surface makes the system state visible and the user's mental model of "the document I am working on" tractable, narrowing the Gulf of Evaluation [Source: academic/don-norman.md §7, citing https://jnd.org/the-design-of-everyday-things-revised-and-expanded/].

Finally, artifacts satisfy Nielsen Heuristic #1 (Visibility of system status — the artifact IS the visible state) and Heuristic #3 (User control and freedom — direct edit + version restore) [Source: academic/jakob-nielsen.md §9, citing https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ten-usability-heuristics/].

## 4. Observed Variations

- **Threshold-based auto-open vs. manual trigger**: ChatGPT (auto at >10 lines or manual `/canvas` slash, "use canvas" prompt, or toolbox icon); Claude (heuristic-only, no auto threshold published; "significant, self-contained, typically >15 lines"); Gemini (no published threshold — appears as a chosen output mode); v0/Lovable/Bolt (artifacts are the product surface itself, not a hoisting behavior). [Source: evidence/chatgpt.md §13; evidence/claude.md §13]
- **Spatial vs. outline-first artifacts**: Heptabase/Obsidian Canvas are spatial 2D canvases; Tana/Reflect/Logseq are outliner-first with no canvas; Craft offers BOTH (whiteboards + documents). [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §13; evidence/tana.md §13; evidence/craft.md §13]
- **Card belongs-to-whiteboard vs. card-on-whiteboard**: Heptabase explicitly states "Cards don't belong to whiteboards — they're placed on them. Same card can be on multiple whiteboards" — a many-to-many spatial model. Obsidian Canvas treats cards more as embeds. [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §13, citing https://wiki.heptabase.com/fundamental-elements]
- **Server-side execution vs. client-side rendering**: Claude runs Python in gVisor server-side container with ephemeral per-session filesystem; renders React/HTML/SVG in client-side sandboxed iframe at claude.site. v0/Lovable/Bolt use WebContainer (client-side dev server sandbox; WebContainer only supports Chromium-family + Firefox + Safari TP, NOT stable Safari — access barrier). [Source: evidence/claude.md §13; evidence/bolt.md §19, citing https://webcontainer.io]
- **Fork granularity**: Lovable Remix (copies code + DB structure + optional chat history; explicitly NOT data/secrets/domains/collaborators); Bolt Duplicate (fresh copy, GitHub/Netlify integrations lost, chat cleared) vs. Transfer (between workspaces); v0 Fork (modal, "Connections preserved when forking" per Jul 7 2026 changelog). [Source: evidence/lovable.md §13; evidence/bolt.md §13; evidence/v0.md §13]
- **Publishing model**: Claude (public vs. org-only Team/Enterprise, embeddable iframe, "publish once" rule — unpublishing permanently deletes storage); Manus (auto-publish blog exists); Craft (custom domains + analytics); Tana (Tana Publish); v0 (Vercel deploy with custom domain). Things3 has NO publish surface — the artifact is personal-only by design. [Source: evidence/claude.md §13; evidence/manus.md §13; evidence/craft.md §13; evidence/tana.md §13; evidence/v0.md §13; evidence/things3.md §13]
- **AI-powered artifacts (stateful)**: Claude allows artifacts to embed Claude's intelligence via text-based API; users authenticate with their own Claude account; 20 MB storage per artifact (personal vs. shared isolation); requires published state. This is unique in the evidence set — a stateful, AI-backed artifact type. [Source: evidence/claude.md §13, citing https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9796387-what-are-artifacts]

## 5. Premium Exemplars (BEST + WHY — evidence-based)

**BEST: Claude Artifacts** — because the design satisfies the most CLT and Nielsen criteria simultaneously: (a) threshold rule (>15 lines) reduces extraneous load by hoisting only significant content; (b) content-type-aware rendering (Documents/Code/HTML/SVG/Mermaid/React) minimizes mode-switching cost; (c) sandboxed iframe with CSP isolation addresses Norman's "feedback + safety" principle; (d) public/org-share distinction + AI-powered stateful artifacts + 20 MB storage extends the artifact lifecycle beyond a single chat; (e) one-way publish (unpublish permanently deletes storage) prevents stale-state ambiguity (Nielsen Heuristic #5 — error prevention). [Source: evidence/claude.md §13, citing https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9796387-what-are-artifacts + https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10012583-publish-and-share-artifacts]

**BEST (tied, for spatial knowledge artifacts): Heptabase Whiteboards** — because the many-to-many card-on-whiteboard model eliminates the duplication extraneous load of "the same note in two folders" by making cards library-owned and whiteboard-referenced; sub-whiteboards give CLT-grounded progressive disclosure by segmentation; mindmap mode provides an alternative representation ( germane load via schema-building through re-representation). [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §13 + §18, citing https://wiki.heptabase.com/fundamental-elements]

**BEST (for code artifacts): v0** — because the four-tab model (Preview / Code / Design / History) provides type-aware surfaces with split-editing, diff views, Cmd+S, plus Design Mode's auto-attached element screenshot for natural-language instructions (reducing the gulf of execution for visual edits), plus version dropdown + fork modal + design-system-as-skill persistence. [Source: evidence/v0.md §13, citing https://v0.dev/docs/code-editing + /design-mode + /versions]

## 6. Anti-Patterns (FAIL + WHY — evidence-based)

- **Safari-read-only limitation in Bolt Code view** — WebContainer only supports stable Chromium-family + Firefox; Safari is read-only for code edits, and stable Safari is unsupported (only Safari TP). This is an access-barrier that violates Nielsen Heuristic #5 (error prevention — the user discovers the limit only after attempting to edit) and creates extraneous load for the Safari user who must context-switch to Chrome. [Source: evidence/bolt.md §19, citing https://support.bolt.new/building/using-bolt/code-view.md + https://webcontainer.io]
- **Claude's "publish once" rule** — Once unpublished, the same artifact CANNOT be published again, and unpublishing permanently deletes associated storage. This is a destructive, irreversible action that violates Norman's "feedback" principle (no recovery path) and Nielsen Heuristic #9 (help users recover from errors). [Source: evidence/claude.md §13, citing https://support.claude.com/en/articles/10012583-publish-and-share-artifacts]
- **ChatGPT Canvas model-gating** — Canvas is unavailable with GPT-5 Pro / GPT-5.2 Pro (the reasoning model variants). Users switching model variants silently lose the artifact surface with no inline explanation in the composer. This violates Heuristic #1 (visibility of system status) and Heuristic #2 (match between system and real world — the user's mental model of "Canvas is always there" is broken). [Source: evidence/chatgpt.md §13, citing https://help.openai.com/en/articles/11909943-gpt-5-in-chatgpt]
- **Lovable long-conversation degradation** — "in very long conversations with a lot of context, instructions may not always be followed consistently" — an artifact that drifts from user intent over long sessions violates Heuristic #4 (consistency and standards) and creates germane-load failure (the user's schema of "what the artifact does" no longer predicts behavior). [Source: evidence/lovable.md §51, citing https://docs.lovable.dev/features/knowledge.md]
- **Manus per-command approval fatigue** — Every Desktop terminal command prompts Allow Once / Always Allow with no per-pattern rule engine; high-frequency CLI workflows become click-through friction that overwhelms the artifact's value. Violates Nielsen Heuristic #7 (flexibility and efficiency of use). [Source: evidence/manus.md §110 + §342-343, citing https://manus.im/docs/features/desktop]
- **Craft's first-class code editor positioning** — "Could this be the best code editor in a writing app?" marketing overstates the use case; embedding a code editor in a writing app risks split-attention between two distinct mental models (writing-as-prose vs. writing-as-code), increasing extraneous load for users who switch contexts. (Counter-evidence: the editor IS first-class per blog Jan 23 2023; the anti-pattern is the marketing positioning rather than the feature.) [Source: evidence/craft.md §13, citing blog.txt Jan 23 2023 entry]

## 7. Cognitive Load Implications (cite CLT)

- **Intrinsic load reduction via hoisting**: ChatGPT's >10-line threshold and Claude's >15-line threshold segment element interactivity — the chat thread becomes a low-element-interactivity surface, while the artifact becomes a high-element-interactivity surface dedicated to the editing schema. This is the CLT **segmentation principle** in product form. [Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §51, citing progressive disclosure as CLT-grounded; evidence/chatgpt.md §13; evidence/claude.md §13]
- **Extraneous load reduction via type-aware rendering**: v0's Design Mode panel (Typography/Color/Layout/Border/Shadow/Content) physically places the relevant control next to the element being edited — eliminating split-attention (CLT split-attention effect, Chandler & Sweller 1991). [Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §30; evidence/v0.md §13]
- **Germane load increase via re-representation**: Tana Views (table/calendar/card/list over the same nodes) and Heptabase mindmap-mode toggle let the user see the same data different ways — schema-building through re-representation is a germane-load-maximizing technique. [Source: evidence/tana.md §13; evidence/heptabase.md §13 + §18]
- **Expertise-reversal risk**: Artifacts that are optimized for novices (e.g., Design Mode visual editor) may slow experts who prefer direct code editing. v0 supports both — Code tab for experts, Design Mode for novices — which is the CLT-recommended mitigation. [Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §44, citing Kalyuga et al. 2003 expertise-reversal effect; evidence/v0.md §13]
- **Multi-agent artifact UIs**: Claude's AI-powered artifacts (stateful apps with Claude intelligence) can present multiple agent states simultaneously; CLT predicts this raises extraneous load — the design addresses it via personal vs. shared storage isolation (segmenting context by owner). [Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §63; evidence/claude.md §13]
- **arXiv:2607.02723 (2026) "Doom Researching"** warns that AI-assisted artifact creation can create an "illusion of knowing" without schema construction (low germane load). Applicable to AI-generated artifacts in v0/Lovable/Bolt where the user may not internalize the generated code. [Source: academic/cognitive-load-theory.md §65, citing https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02723]

## 8. Progressive Disclosure Relationship

Artifacts and progressive disclosure are tightly coupled in the evidence set:

- **Claude's Agent Skills explicitly use 3-level progressive disclosure** — at startup, only skill name + description loaded; second level is the skill body; third level (and beyond) is bundled files Claude navigates on-demand. Artifacts inherit this pattern: content >15 lines is hoisted to a side panel (level 1); version history is hidden behind a selector (level 2); AI-powered stateful storage is hidden behind publishing (level 3). [Source: evidence/claude.md §18, citing https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/equipping-agents-for-the-real-world-with-agent-skills]
- **Heptabase sub-whiteboards** are progressive disclosure by spatial nesting — zoom into a sub-topic without leaving the main whiteboard context. [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §18]
- **Tana zoom-in** to a node (focus mode) is classic outliner progressive disclosure (matching Roam/Logseq). [Source: evidence/tana.md §18]
- **ChatGPT Canvas shortcuts** appear as a top toolbar ONLY when Canvas is open — not in the main chat composer. This is the canonical "show advanced controls only when relevant" pattern. [Source: evidence/chatgpt.md §18]
- **Nielsen (2006)** empirically claims progressive disclosure improves learnability, efficiency, and error rate — and that the "limiting mental model" concern is groundless. The artifact pattern operationalizes this: the chat surface is the "primary" task; the artifact is the "advanced" surface revealed on demand. [Source: academic/progressive-disclosure.md §39, citing https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/]

## 9. Accessibility Considerations

- **Craft** has the most explicitly considered a11y posture: keyboard-only access via `Ctrl+Return` context-menu shortcut (improves accessibility AND keyboard-first workflows); external keyboard support on iOS/iPadOS when connected. [Source: evidence/craft.md §19, citing introduction_shortcuts.md]
- **Obsidian** changelog mentions keyboard navigation improvements in Settings (Tab/Shift-Tab focusable elements; Escape closes Settings). Forum-documented screen-reader limitations and color-blind issues were NOT fetched — gap noted. [Source: evidence/obsidian.md §19, citing https://obsidian.md/changelog/2026-08-05-desktop-v1.13.5/]
- **Claude** marketing pages support `focus-visible` outlines with configurable `--focus--width` / `--focus--offset-outer` CSS variables; provides localized hreflang alternates (en-US, ja-JP, de-DE, fr-FR, ko-KR, it-IT); anti-flicker CSS with `<noscript>` fallback. Help center offers 12 languages. Visual content has explicit mobile limitations (weather as text on mobile). [Source: evidence/claude.md §19]
- **v0** has no published VPAT/WCAG conformance statement. iOS app exists but MCP server presets are desktop-only. Mobile settings page regression fix (May 15 2026) and "sidebar no longer flashes on load on narrow screens" (Jul 31 2026) suggest iterative mobile-a11y improvement. [Source: evidence/v0.md §19]
- **Bolt** has no documented keyboard shortcut set beyond `Ctrl+S`/`Enter`; Safari read-only limitation is an access-barrier. No VPAT/WCAG statement, no documented screen-reader behavior for chat thread, no high-contrast or reduced-motion mode. Weakest a11y posture in evidence set. [Source: evidence/bolt.md §19]
- **Lovable** surfaces accessibility issues via its SEO & AI search tool (implies auto-checks for some a11y issues in generated apps); browser testing checks behavior across screen sizes. No published VPAT/WCAG statement. [Source: evidence/lovable.md §19]
- **Heptabase / Tana** — no a11y statement found on fetched pages. Heptabase wiki uses Docusaurus (has skip-to-main-content link by default). Tana's outliner is heavily mouse-driven (drag, hover menus). Insufficient evidence — gap noted. [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §19; evidence/tana.md §19]

## 10. Performance Implications

- **Claude artifacts** render React/HTML/SVG in a client-side iframe with `loading="lazy"` attribute — defers off-screen artifact rendering. Server-side Python runs in gVisor container with ephemeral per-session filesystem (no persistent client state). 20 MB per-artifact storage cap. [Source: evidence/claude.md §13]
- **v0 / Lovable / Bolt** all use WebContainer (client-side Node.js runtime in browser via WebAssembly). WebContainer only supports Chromium-family + Firefox + Safari TP — stable Safari is unsupported, creating both an access barrier AND a performance cliff for Safari users. [Source: evidence/bolt.md §19, citing https://webcontainer.io]
- **Obsidian** claims performance at 10k notes (its marketing uses this as a benchmark) — local-first architecture with file-system-based storage means performance scales with disk I/O, not server round-trips. [Source: evidence/obsidian.md §20]
- **Heptabase** whiteboards are spatial 2D — pan/zoom performance depends on browser canvas rendering. No published performance benchmarks. [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §20 — Insufficient evidence — gap noted]
- **Things3** uses calm visual design as a long-session performance strategy: "Things never feels messy or overbearing, no matter the length of your task list" achieved through whitespace, limited chrome, animation that "keep[s] your place" — perceived-performance optimization. [Source: evidence/things3.md §297, citing https://culturedcode.com/things/]
- **ChatGPT Canvas** Markdown-only formatting (no advanced formatting) is itself a performance decision — restricting the rendering surface to a small Markdown subset keeps the editor responsive. [Source: evidence/chatgpt.md §13]

## 11. Long-Session Impact

- **Claude** — memory persistence across sessions (real-time read/write entries, not 24-hour-scheduled) means artifact state survives chats. Settings > Reflect monthly recap surfaces long-session patterns (topics, peak hours, observations). Settings > Time and focus for break reminders. [Source: evidence/claude.md §9 + §23]
- **Lovable** — explicit long-session limitation: "in very long conversations with a lot of context, instructions may not always be followed consistently." Artifact drift is a documented risk. [Source: evidence/lovable.md §51]
- **Heptabase** — visual/spatial nature of whiteboards mitigates outliner fatigue (the Tana/Logseq weakness) — long sessions benefit from spatial reasoning's lower working-memory demand vs. single-pane indentation-heavy outliners. [Source: evidence/heptabase.md §199]
- **Tana** — outliner fatigue is a known risk (single-pane, indentation-heavy); mitigations: daily notes reset focus, search nodes reduce need to navigate, views re-project the same data. [Source: evidence/tana.md §222 + §265]
- **Things3** — Inbox reflex is the anti-fatigue mechanism: "immediately getting them off your mind and into the Inbox to revisit later. When this becomes a reflex, it's also a lot easier to stay focused on whatever you're doing." Calm visual design prevents list-length overwhelm. [Source: evidence/things3.md §297, citing https://culturedcode.com/things/guide/ + https://culturedcode.com/things/]
- **Manus** — per-command approval fatigue is the core long-session cost. Per-session approval fatigue (Browser Operator requires re-authorization every browser task) compounds this. [Source: evidence/manus.md §110 + §248-251]
- **Craft** — External Locations / Keep on Device Folders for offline-first long sessions (no network dependence). [Source: evidence/craft.md §382]
- **Fantastical** — Calendar Sets for context switching without fatigue. [Source: evidence/fantastical.md §255]
