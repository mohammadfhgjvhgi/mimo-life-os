## 3. Core Mental Model

Superhuman Mail's mental model is **inbox-as-task-list with split-by-priority**:
- **Split Inbox**: "With Split Inbox, you can instead focus on what needs your attention. Split out emails from your team, your VIPs, or your most used tools — Google Docs, Notion, Asana — or anything else." [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **Auto Labels** (AI-driven): "With Auto Labels, Superhuman AI will automatically separate marketing, cold pitches, and social network updates, so you can respond faster to what matters most. You can even make your own Auto Labels with short AI prompts like 'job applications' or 'requests to review work'." [Source: https://superhuman.com/ai, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **Auto Archive** (AI-driven): "Superhuman AI can automatically archive marketing, cold pitches, and social network updates." [Source: https://superhuman.com/ai, accessed 2026-08-07]

The mental model treats email as a workflow surface, not just a message store. Three pillars:
1. **Triage** (Auto Labels, Auto Archive, Split Inbox)
2. **Reply** (Instant Reply, Write with Voice, Snippets, Send Later)
3. **Follow-up** (Follow-up reminders, Auto Drafts 2.0)

[Source: https://superhuman.com/ai, accessed 2026-08-07]

For the broader Superhuman suite, the mental model expands to **AI-assisted work surface**: "Like a truly great assistant, Superhuman AI is constantly working beside you. Organizing your inbox. Making sure you never drop the ball. Drafting — and if you want — sending fully written emails on your behalf. It can even execute complete workflows, end-to-end, so you don't have to." [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]

## 14. Keyboard UX (DEEP)

The official `help.superhuman.com` keyboard shortcuts page was inaccessible (Cloudflare challenge + 404 on alternate URLs). [Observed: https://help.superhuman.com returned "Just a moment..." Cloudflare challenge; https://help.superhuman.com/article/keyboard-shortcuts returned 404; https://help.superhuman.com/en/articles/keyboard-shortcuts returned 404, accessed 2026-08-07]

Indirect evidence of keyboard-first design:
- **"Fly through emails with shortcuts"** as a listed feature on the Mail product page (no specific shortcuts enumerated). [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **"Autocorrect increases typing speed by 30-50%"** — keyboard-input speed optimization. [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **Calendar shortcut** referenced without specific key: "Looking busy? Just hit a shortcut, and we'll show the next day." [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **Snippets** are typically invoked via keyboard shortcuts in email clients (not explicitly documented here). [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **Command-palette-style inputs** in the homepage demo: "Schedule 15 minutes for a quick meeting with Mike" / "Tailor this language for executives" — these read like keyboard-invoked commands. [Source: https://superhuman.com, accessed 2026-08-07]
- **Send Later** and **Snooze** are typically keyboard-shortcut-driven in email apps. [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]

The product is widely known to be keyboard-first (third-party coverage consistently cites `⌘K` as the command palette trigger), but **the official sources fetched here do not document the specific shortcut set**. This is a documentation gap rather than an absence of the feature.

**Autocorrect as keyboard UX acceleration**: "Speed up your typing by 30-50% Autocorrect fixes errors as you go — extra characters, missing characters, transposed characters, missing punctuation, incorrect capitalization — to name just a few. It beats the autocorrect in Gmail and Outlook across multiple benchmarks. Most importantly, you go faster. Autocorrect increases typing speed by 30-50%." [Source: https://superhuman.com/products/mail, accessed 2026-08-07]

This is a notable pattern: Superhuman competes on **typing speed itself** as a UX dimension, not just on shortcuts.
